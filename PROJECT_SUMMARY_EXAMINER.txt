==================== PAGE 001 OF 100 ====================
TITLE AND PROJECT IDENTITY

Equimaster-Ai is a Streamlit stock-research dashboard for Indian-market
companies. It brings market overview, stock screening, price charts, LSTM
forecasting, company fundamentals, news retrieval, and evidence-linked
sentiment into one research interface. Backend scripts support data refresh,
training, evaluation, and scheduled operations.

The system is a research and decision-support tool, not an automated trading
system. Recommendations and market summaries are heuristic; forecasts are
uncertain; news sources can be incomplete. The interface should be interpreted
as a convenient view of available evidence and model outputs, not as
investment advice or a guarantee of future returns.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Equimaster-Ai organizes multiple stock-research tasks in one application.
Users can inspect market breadth and movers, search a coverage universe,
review a company, chart price history, request LSTM forecasts, view financial
statements and ratios, and inspect timestamped news evidence. A backend
pipeline maintains data and versioned models.

Core model operations are designed around chronology, persistence baselines,
immutable bundles, and explicit limitations. News records preserve
publication and observation times for historical cutoff checks. A learned
Ridge fusion experiment combines an LSTM return feature with news sentiment,
LLM stance, and horizon. Its inference is guarded and remains a separately
labeled shadow candidate, not the production forecast.

==================== PAGE 005 OF 100 ====================
ABSTRACT

This project implements a financial-market research dashboard focused on
Indian equities. Its main components are a market landing page and screener,
company analysis sections, technical charts, direct multi-session LSTM
forecasting, fundamentals and peer comparisons, news normalization and
retrieval, deterministic sentiment scoring, optional structured LLM
synthesis, evaluation utilities, and backend refresh workflows.

The system emphasizes inspectable evidence and model provenance. Forecasts
are evaluated against persistence; data splits are chronological; overlapping
forecast targets are purged at boundaries; and model artifacts retain
metadata. The project also contains an experimental fusion dataset, model,
artifact contract, inference guard, staged-trial gate, and opt-in UI display.
It does not yet demonstrate that fusion improves generalizable accuracy.

==================== PAGE 006 OF 100 ====================
PROJECT MOTIVATION

Market research often requires switching among price histories, company
financials, news feeds, and model outputs. That fragmentation makes it harder
to compare evidence at a consistent point in time. Equimaster-Ai provides a
single dashboard that groups these research surfaces around a selected
company while retaining market-wide context.

The project also addresses a common presentation problem: a forecast number
without a baseline or provenance can appear more certain than it is. The
system therefore surfaces measured metrics, historical origins, target dates,
source details, and experimental labels where available. It prioritizes
traceability and honest status reporting over promotional accuracy claims.

==================== PAGE 007 OF 100 ====================
PROBLEM STATEMENT

The engineering problem is to assemble market, company, and news information
into a usable application while preserving enough provenance to evaluate
forecasts fairly. Data may be missing, delayed, corrected, or reported in
different currencies. Headlines can be ambiguous. Historical evidence must
not leak information observed after a forecast origin.

The modeling problem is separate: a usable interface does not imply that an
LSTM beats a simple persistence forecast, or that sentiment improves it. The
project therefore treats interface delivery, data reliability, and predictive
validity as distinct dimensions. Each requires separate tests and evidence.

==================== PAGE 008 OF 100 ====================
PROJECT OBJECTIVES

The project objectives are to provide a coherent stock-research workflow;
make market and company data easier to inspect; generate horizon-based LSTM
forecasts; provide deterministic news sentiment with traceable evidence;
preserve historical cutoff information; and support reproducible training
and evaluation through backend workflows.

An additional objective is to study whether a learned fusion of price-model
and news/LLM features can improve forecast diagnostics. This objective is
experimental. It is pursued with schema validation, chronological splits,
artifact provenance, guarded inference, and a conservative evaluation gate.
No objective implies a guaranteed trading return.

NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Important quality requirements include reproducibility, temporal correctness,

SYSTEM USERS AND USE CASES

The primary user is a researcher or student exploring covered equities. The
user can start from market context, filter companies, open a company page,
review historical prices and indicators, request an LSTM forecast, inspect
news sentiment, and compare selected financial measures. CSV and JSON
exports can support independent analysis.

An operator or developer uses command-line scripts to refresh data, train
models, evaluate watchlists, review logs, or install the scheduled task. The
current watchlist is held in the Streamlit session; it is not a persistent
account service. Administrative training remains a backend operation, not a
frontend Training Manager.

==================== PAGE 013 OF 100 ====================
HIGH-LEVEL ARCHITECTURE

The runtime architecture has three cooperating paths. Market data flows
through download, normalization, preprocessing, and LSTM inference. News
flows through source retrieval, normalization, archival, chunking, retrieval,
and sentiment. Company data flows through public sources, snapshot caching,
calculation, and comparison.

The Streamlit interface connects those paths into market and company pages.
Separate scripts handle recurring refresh and training. The fusion research
path joins aligned LSTM backtest rows to point-in-time news/LLM features,
splits the dataset chronologically, fits a model, stores an immutable
artifact, and exposes guarded experimental inference.

==================== PAGE 014 OF 100 ====================
TECHNOLOGY AND RUNTIME

The project is Python-based and uses Streamlit for the interface. The
documented environment is Windows with Python 3.13. Core dependencies are
pinned through requirements files, with optional requirements for development,
news models, and browser UI tooling. The application can run with the core
sentiment fallback and TF-IDF retrieval without optional model downloads.

Modeling uses TensorFlow/Keras for LSTM inference/training, scikit-learn for
preprocessing and the Ridge fusion experiment, and joblib for serialized
artifacts. Pandas and NumPy support data processing; Plotly renders charts.
External market, news, company-financial, and optional LLM services are
configured at runtime.

Market-session status is calculated using the Indian exchange trading

indicator is not evidence that every quote is live.

STOCK SCREENER

The documented screener filters the saved company coverage by sector, name,
P/E, ROE, debt/equity, or recent returns. Search results can be exported,
and company links lead to the associated research page. Generic leverage
screens exclude financial companies because their balance-sheet ratios are
not directly comparable with industrial firms.

Screen results are constrained by the coverage universe and freshness of
underlying data. A filter match is not a recommendation or a suitability
assessment. The screen is a navigation and discovery aid that should be used
with source periods and company-specific context.

The technical chart supports date-range selection and daily, weekly, or

These indicators summarize historical market behavior. They are not

FORECAST USER EXPERIENCE

The LSTM section allows a user to select Day, Week, Month, or Quarter and choose
Direct or Recursive forecasting. It presents a predicted close, return,
combined signal, and news evidence count. When available, it displays
empirical intervals and historical model-performance metrics.

Forecast results are retained in Streamlit session state across reruns when
the ticker, horizon, strategy, model artifact, data origin, and experimental
fusion configuration match. The page supports CSV export. The existing LSTM
result remains the authoritative user-facing forecast; a fusion candidate
has separate labeling and behavior.

==================== PAGE 025 OF 100 ====================
FORECAST HORIZONS AND CONTRACT

Supported periods are Day (1 session), Week (5), Month (22), and Quarter (88).
Quarter retains the existing 88-session model and spans approximately four
months. They are trading-session counts, not calendar-day durations. The forecast
origin is after the close of the most recent available session, and the
target is the Close at the horizon endpoint.

The persistence baseline predicts the latest observed close. This simple
benchmark is important because a complex model should not be presumed
superior. Exchange holidays and session calendars must be aligned to actual
market data rather than guessed from ordinary calendar arithmetic.

==================== PAGE 026 OF 100 ====================
DIRECT AND RECURSIVE FORECASTS

Direct forecasting requests the model output for the selected horizon and
checks that the output length matches the requested number of future
sessions. Recursive forecasting repeatedly estimates the next value,
updates a synthetic OHLC row, recomputes technical indicators, and feeds the
result back into the model for additional steps.

Recursive results accumulate model error and make assumptions about future
market fields. The current interface does not expose recursive forecasting;
its historical metrics and intervals refer to direct models.

==================== PAGE 027 OF 100 ====================
FORECAST INFERENCE CONTRACT

`src/forecast.py` resolves a ticker/horizon model artifact, verifies that
input columns match the model feature contract, checks that sufficient
history exists, loads the matching scaler and model, and scales the input
window before prediction. It rejects non-finite indicators and mismatched
direct output length.

The target Close is recovered from the model's scaling range. Artifact
metadata carries feature order and window size for versioned models. Legacy
models have less provenance and are explicitly treated as legacy by the
artifact resolver and interface.

==================== PAGE 029 OF 100 ====================
MARKET DATA INGESTION

Market-data scripts download source data and create processed company
datasets. The documented daily pipeline refreshes raw data, computes
indicators, trains changed datasets, evaluates active bundles, refreshes
news, and updates stale company fundamentals. Separate scripts support
targeted downloads and model repair or training tasks.

Provider data can be missing, revised, or rounded differently between
downloads. The training documentation reports that tiny feed-rounding
changes are ignored to avoid unnecessary retraining, while material
corrections trigger retraining. Failed refreshes are not supposed to replace
the active model bundle.

==================== PAGE 030 OF 100 ====================
PREPROCESSING AND INDICATORS

Processed market rows are prepared with technical features before LSTM
training. The sequencing utility can create historical input windows and
multi-step targets. The canonical training pipeline performs its own
chronological splits from processed CSV data rather than relying on exported
arrays as the source of truth.

Indicators and columns form part of the model contract. Input features must
match the saved model's expected feature set. A changed feature definition
requires compatible training artifacts; silently reordering or adding
columns would undermine reproducibility.

==================== PAGE 031 OF 100 ====================
LSTM MODEL ROLE

The LSTM is the principal learned price-forecasting model in the production
dashboard. The training documentation describes it as a residual model that
learns corrections to the last observed close. Each ticker and horizon has
an evaluated model artifact where available.

The model uses a window of historical features, with a scaler fit only on
the fitting partition. Forecasting does not use news as an LSTM input tensor
in the current pipeline. News and technical context contribute to separate
heuristic signals, while the learned fusion experiment is maintained as an
independent model path.

==================== PAGE 032 OF 100 ====================
TRAINING PIPELINE

All model-training entrypoints delegate to `src/training.py`, with
`scripts/train_manager.py` serving as the canonical bulk-management
interface. The documented daily pipeline trains when input datasets have
changed. One-ticker and selected-horizon runs are available through helper
scripts.

Training uses fitting, calibration/validation, and test periods in
chronological order. The scaler is fit on fitting rows only. The
documentation records model parameters, seed, data boundaries, data hash,
and model versions to support later reproduction and audit.

==================== PAGE 033 OF 100 ====================
CHRONOLOGICAL SPLITS AND LEAKAGE

Forecast evaluation must preserve the order of time. Training and later
partitions cannot be randomly mixed because future prices would then
influence model fitting or tuning. Multi-session labels overlap across
adjacent origins, so rows whose targets cross a split boundary must be
purged.

The project documentation describes purged chronological fitting,
validation, and test boundaries for LSTM models. The fusion experiment
likewise uses global chronological origin boundaries and purges rows whose
target close would not yet be observable at the next partition boundary.

==================== PAGE 034 OF 100 ====================
MODEL BUNDLES AND PROVENANCE

Versioned model bundles are stored under model directories and include
models, scaler, feature order, metadata, processed-data snapshot, hashes,
training dates, evaluations, and backtests. An active pointer selects the
bundle used for normal inference. Publishing is intended to be atomic so a
failed training run does not leave a partial active model.

Older `.h5` or `.keras` files remain available through a legacy fallback.
Legacy artifacts do not receive invented training dates or performance
metrics. Artifact containment and checksums are validated in the relevant
loading paths.

==================== PAGE 035 OF 100 ====================
MODEL SELECTION AND BASELINES

The dashboard compares each LSTM model with persistence. Training and
validation choices are separated from held-out test reporting. The README
warns that a baseline can have lower MAE than an LSTM and that the display
should report this honestly.

The 24 October status report records that 25 of 57 Day, 30 of 57 Week,
22 of 57 Month, and 21 of 57 Quarter models beat persistence MAE
(periods are 1, 5, 22, and 88 sessions). This demonstrates that performance is mixed across
the reported universe; aggregate dashboard access does not imply consistent
model superiority.

==================== PAGE 036 OF 100 ====================
FORECAST METRICS

The project uses MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and directional accuracy. MAE and RMSE
measure price error in the unit of the security, while MAPE expresses
relative error. Directional accuracy measures whether the predicted price
movement direction agrees with the realized direction under the project's
forecast contract.

Price errors should be compared within ticker and horizon because securities
have different price scales. The fusion evaluation reports per-ticker and
per-horizon results and macro-averages rather than pooling raw price errors
across differently priced assets.

==================== PAGE 037 OF 100 ====================
REPORTED LSTM PERFORMANCE

The 24 October status report provides unweighted means across 57 tickers.
It reports mean directional accuracy of 50.34% for Day, 49.86% for Week,
48.41% for Month, and 46.53% for Quarter (1, 5, 22, and 88 sessions). The report also

models, or the evaluation period changes.

==================== PAGE 038 OF 100 ====================
UNCERTAINTY INTERVALS

Direct-model empirical intervals are formed from validation residual
quantiles, with interval coverage measured on test data. The interface
displays measured coverage and describes the intervals as empirical.
Validation also participates in early-stopping selection, so the intervals
are not presented as guaranteed conformal prediction intervals.

The reported nominal interval target is 90%, but documented test coverage
was 76.33%, 75.71%, 67.83%, and 37.55% for horizons 1, 5, 22, and 88.
The substantial long-horizon shortfall is a clear calibration research
item.

==================== PAGE 039 OF 100 ====================
LIMITATIONS OF HISTORICAL BACKTESTS

The README and status report distinguish rolling predictions from a
repeatedly refitted walk-forward study. The documented backtests use a
fixed fitted model and observed prior prices as rolling input windows.
Repeated retraining at each historical origin has not been established as
the same evaluation.

The current reports do not establish transaction-cost-adjusted performance,
market impact, liquidity constraints, short-sale feasibility, or realized
portfolio returns. A forecast metric is therefore not sufficient evidence
for a deployable investment strategy.

==================== PAGE 040 OF 100 ====================
NEWS AND SENTIMENT PIPELINE

The news path retrieves articles, normalizes metadata, matches content to
tickers, archives observations, chunks long text, retrieves relevant
evidence, and computes sentiment. Evidence records retain sources and
timestamps used by downstream display and historical evaluation.

News is an independent evidence stream from the LSTM. The deterministic
sentiment score is not a direct LSTM feature in the normal production
forecast. The market summary and combined signal use heuristics. The learned
fusion path consumes selected news and LLM-derived features only when the
experimental prerequisites are met.

==================== PAGE 041 OF 100 ====================
NEWS SOURCES AND COVERAGE

Documented sources include Yahoo and public publisher RSS feeds, with
optional NewsAPI coverage when `NEWS_API_KEY` is configured. The RSS
fallback is finite and does not contain every company every day. Public
sources may change their page structure or feed availability.

The status report states that the latest verified refresh returned articles
for 9 of 57 configured symbols. That snapshot indicates a significant
coverage limitation; it should not be generalized as the current coverage
without a new refresh. Missing coverage is preserved as missing evidence
rather than replaced with a fabricated article.

==================== PAGE 042 OF 100 ====================
NEWS NORMALIZATION

News normalization records ticker, source, title, summary or text, URLs,
publication time, and observation time where available. Missing publication
times are retained as missing instead of being replaced with the retrieval
time. This distinction matters because retrieval time does not prove when
an article first became available.

Feed timestamps are normalized to UTC. Publication times can be displayed
in India time for users. Canonical URL handling removes tracking parameters
for identity and deduplication while preserving original and canonical
provenance.

==================== PAGE 043 OF 100 ====================
TICKER AND COMPANY MATCHING

The news system uses configured ticker and company-name aliases. Short
symbol matches require financial context to reduce ambiguous matches, since
many ticker strings also appear as ordinary words. This is an important
protection against assigning an unrelated article to a company.

Matching is evidence association, not a guarantee that a story materially
affects the security. Company names can overlap with products, people, or
other entities. A regional independently labeled dataset is still needed
to measure matching precision and recall under Indian-market news conditions.

==================== PAGE 044 OF 100 ====================
ARCHIVAL AND CUTOFF SAFETY

Fresh news observations are archived under `data/news_archive/` for future
historical studies. Historical retrieval requires both publication and
observation timestamps at or before the requested forecast origin. An
article published earlier but first observed after the origin is not
eligible for a point-in-time reconstruction.

Historical sentiment requires explicitly archived articles and raises when
no eligible archive exists; it does not silently fall back to live news.
This policy reduces look-ahead leakage but depends on archive completeness
and trustworthy timestamp provenance.

==================== PAGE 045 OF 100 ====================
CHUNKING AND PROVENANCE

Long articles are divided into chunks that preserve article and ticker
identity, source, original/canonical URLs, publication and observation
times, and duplicate-source provenance. Stable chunk identifiers are
derived from canonical article identity and chunk position/content.

Stable IDs let evaluation and LLM synthesis refer to specific evidence.
Metadata-only changes can be refreshed without forcing re-embedding of
unchanged text. Provenance is essential for locating a citation and applying
historical cutoff checks.

==================== PAGE 046 OF 100 ====================
DEDUPLICATION

URL tracking variants and near-duplicate headlines are deduplicated using
canonical identity and same-day matching rules documented by the project.
Source and URL provenance are retained rather than discarded, allowing
multiple publishers or source records to remain auditable.

Deduplication prevents repeated copies of a story from dominating retrieval
and metrics. However, overly aggressive deduplication could merge distinct
updates; identity thresholds and canonicalization rules therefore remain
part of evaluation quality.

==================== PAGE 047 OF 100 ====================
RETRIEVAL

The retriever ranks article chunks against a ticker-specific query and can
apply recency and as-of constraints. TF-IDF retrieval is the core option;
embedding-based retrieval is optional. Retrieval returns evidence metadata,
not merely text, so citations and source context can be displayed.

Retrieval should be measured with a labeled set using article-level
Precision@K, Recall@K, mean reciprocal rank, and normalized discounted
cumulative gain. The repository contains metric utilities, but the project
status warns that a tiny synthetic retrieval smoke test is not a real-world
benchmark.

==================== PAGE 048 OF 100 ====================
OPTIONAL EMBEDDINGS

Embedding retrieval can be enabled with compatible `sentence-transformers`
and `faiss-cpu` packages and the `EQUIMASTER_EMBEDDINGS` setting. It is
optional; TF-IDF retrieval remains available without those dependencies.
No model download is required by default.

Optional embeddings can improve semantic matching in some conditions, but
their presence does not automatically improve retrieval quality. Corpus
coverage, ticker matching, chunk boundaries, model choice, index freshness,
and a labeled retrieval evaluation all influence performance.

==================== PAGE 049 OF 100 ====================
DETERMINISTIC SENTIMENT

The default sentiment pipeline scores retrieved articles with FinBERT when
the optional model is available and enabled; otherwise it uses a keyword
fallback. Relevance and source weights contribute to a composite ticker
score. The output also includes label, confidence, drivers, article-level
scores, and impact indicators.

These scores are heuristics unless calibrated and independently validated.
The status report explicitly says confidence and impact scores are not
calibrated probabilities. A displayed score should not be interpreted as
the probability that a stock will rise or fall.

==================== PAGE 050 OF 100 ====================
FINBERT AND OPTIONAL MODEL DOWNLOADS

FinBERT dependencies and model use are optional. The README gives a
separate `requirements-news.txt` installation path and environment flags.
The core application can run using keyword sentiment. Model downloads are
disabled unless explicitly permitted by configuration.

The runtime catches model-load failures and falls back to deterministic
keyword scoring. This keeps the app usable offline but can change the
sentiment method used for a particular result. The backend identifies the
scoring backend in article-level output where available.

==================== PAGE 051 OF 100 ====================
KEYWORD SENTIMENT AND BENCHMARK

The status report records evaluation of the default keyword scorer on
2,264 expert-labeled FinancialPhraseBank sentences. It reports 69.61%
accuracy, 49.39% balanced accuracy, macro F1 of 0.507, and negative-example
recall of 15.84%. The majority-class baseline accuracy was 61.44%.

The weak negative recall is material. FinancialPhraseBank is not an
Indian-market timestamped news corpus, and a pretrained model's training
data must be checked before calling this an unseen test. The result is a
benchmark snapshot, not a general guarantee of headline accuracy.

==================== PAGE 052 OF 100 ====================
SENTIMENT DISPLAY

The News and Sentiment interface presents the deterministic sentiment
result, source evidence, publication information, and optional structured
LLM synthesis. It reports when synthesis is disabled, unavailable, or
failed. Provider errors do not replace the deterministic sentiment score
with a synthetic result.

Evidence links allow a user to inspect cited sources. Displayed citations
provide traceability, but they do not automatically prove semantic
entailment. A human should verify important claims against the original
article and its full context.

==================== PAGE 053 OF 100 ====================
LLM PROVIDER BOUNDARY

The optional LLM adapter supports OpenAI-compatible chat-completions
endpoints. The provider is disabled by default. Configuration includes
provider mode, base URL, model, optional API key, and request timeout.
Hosted endpoints must use HTTPS; loopback HTTP is permitted for local
inference services.

API keys are read from the process environment and should not be committed
to source control or written into logs. An external hosted provider receives
article text sent for synthesis; operators should consider data handling,
provider retention, and organizational policy before enabling it.

==================== PAGE 054 OF 100 ====================
STRUCTURED LLM SYNTHESIS

The synthesis function receives ticker-matched retrieved chunks and an
explicit `as_of` cutoff. It rejects missing evidence, invalid timestamps,
wrong-ticker records, and evidence published or observed after the cutoff.
The requested result contains stance, summary, claims, and citation IDs.

The output schema is validated, and citations must refer to supplied
evidence chunk identifiers. This is a structural check: it ensures that
the cited IDs exist in the input but cannot establish that the generated
claim is fully supported by the cited text.

==================== PAGE 055 OF 100 ====================
PROMPT AND UNTRUSTED INPUTS

Retrieved news text is untrusted external content. The provider prompt
instructs the model not to follow instructions embedded in article text and
to synthesize only from supplied evidence. Output is constrained to a
structured JSON object that the application validates.

Prompt instructions and JSON validation reduce some risks but do not
eliminate hallucination, prompt-injection influence, or unsupported
paraphrase. Human review and independent label-based evaluation remain
important before using LLM output in high-impact decisions.

==================== PAGE 056 OF 100 ====================
LLM EVALUATION

The LLM evaluation utility accepts an explicitly injected provider and
independently labeled cases. It reports stance accuracy and macro F1, plus
citation precision, recall, and F1 against labeled evidence IDs. Invalid
gold evidence identifiers are rejected before model calls.

Citation overlap is not a semantic entailment metric, and stance quality
does not establish forecast accuracy. A useful evaluation set must be
representative, timestamped where historical use is intended, independently
annotated, and separated from prompts or examples used for development.

==================== PAGE 057 OF 100 ====================
NEWS EVALUATION

The project supports article-level retrieval metrics and sentiment
benchmark evaluation. It can evaluate labeled sentiment cases and compare
against a majority-class baseline. The synthetic fixture provides an
offline smoke test for expected behavior, not real-world performance
evidence.

Regional studies should use independently labeled Indian-market articles,
including publication and first-observed timestamps. Class balance,
language, company matching, source variation, and recency should be
reported so aggregate scores do not hide important failure modes.

==================== PAGE 058 OF 100 ====================
FUSION RESEARCH MOTIVATION

The fusion research path studies whether news features and LLM stance can
add information beyond an existing LSTM return forecast. It is deliberately
separated from the established LSTM pipeline to avoid changing already
trained model inputs or silently altering normal forecasts.

The hypothesis is empirical, not assumed. News features may be noisy,
incomplete, delayed, or redundant with price history. The fusion model must
be compared with both LSTM and persistence on chronological held-out data
before any expansion of its role.

==================== PAGE 059 OF 100 ====================
FUSION DATASET CONTRACT

The versioned fusion row binds ticker, UTC origin, horizon, target date,
current close, LSTM-predicted close, realized close, heuristic news score,
LLM stance, cited evidence IDs, timestamped evidence, and LLM/LSTM model
identifiers. Validation rejects invalid fields, duplicate samples, missing
provenance, and evidence outside the permitted cutoff.

This schema makes the forecast origin and target explicit. Its target date
must come from aligned market data; it does not infer exchange holidays
from calendar dates. The row contract supports reproducible joining and
evaluation rather than live feature collection by itself.

==================== PAGE 060 OF 100 ====================
FUSION FEATURE CONSTRUCTION

The initial learned fusion model uses LSTM log-return, heuristic news
sentiment, mapped LLM stance, and log horizon. The target is realized
log-return. Bullish stance maps to positive one, bearish to negative one,
and neutral or mixed to zero.

Log returns allow the model to learn relative movement rather than raw
price scale. The design remains small and fixed to limit complexity in the
initial experiment. More inputs or nonlinear models should not be added
without sufficient data and a predeclared evaluation design.

==================== PAGE 061 OF 100 ====================
ALIGNMENT OF FORECAST AND EVIDENCE

`build_fusion_dataset` joins LSTM backtest forecasts to news feature rows
using exact normalized ticker and origin. A missing feature row is an error
rather than an invitation to use today's live evidence. Duplicate
ticker/origin feature rows are rejected.

Exact origin matching avoids accidental alignment to a nearby date. This
design is essential because even a one-session offset may associate news
with a different information set. Evidence timestamps remain attached to
each generated fusion record.

==================== PAGE 062 OF 100 ====================
CHRONOLOGICAL FUSION SPLIT

`chronological_purged_split` applies global date boundaries across tickers
and horizons, by default allocating 60% to training, 20% to validation,
and 20% to test origins. It purges training or validation rows whose target
would not yet have been observed at the following boundary.

Global boundaries reduce cross-ticker time leakage. The split still depends
on correct origin and target dates and adequate coverage across partitions.
The resulting evaluation is only as reliable as its timestamped input
dataset and its adherence to the declared contract.

==================== PAGE 063 OF 100 ====================
FUSION BASELINES

The baseline evaluator includes persistence, LSTM-only, news-combined,
LLM-stance-only, and fixed-rule LSTM-plus-LLM results. It uses a stated
0.6/0.4 combined-signal rule and reports per-ticker/horizon metrics with
macro-averages.

The fixed-rule baselines are diagnostic and are not trained fusion models.
They do not constitute a chronological holdout unless the input rows have
been split appropriately. The report must not present a positive baseline
comparison as proof of future accuracy.

==================== PAGE 064 OF 100 ====================
LEARNED FUSION MODEL

The first learned model is a standardized Ridge regressor. Its scaler and
regressor are fit on training rows only. Validation and test rows are
scored separately; their outcomes do not contribute to fitting. A focused
test checks that changing validation or test labels does not change fitted
coefficients.

Ridge regression provides a relatively simple, inspectable initial model.
It does not eliminate risks from small samples, changing regimes, feature
misspecification, model-selection bias, or repeated use of a held-out test.
Performance must be assessed across additional untouched periods.

==================== PAGE 065 OF 100 ====================
FUSION ARTIFACTS

Fusion artifacts are stored in immutable version bundles. Metadata records
schema and feature versions, stance mapping, model IDs by split, dataset
hash, split boundaries, sample counts, model version, and evaluation
metrics. The estimator file is checksummed.

The loader checks the artifact version and containment, feature contract,
schema, file name, and checksum. Joblib files contain executable pickle
data; only locally created and controlled artifacts should be loaded.
There is no active fusion pointer or automatic latest-version selection.

==================== PAGE 066 OF 100 ====================
GUARDED FUSION INFERENCE

`predict_fused_forecast` validates the loaded artifact contract, ticker,
origin, supported horizon, prices, score range, stance, evidence IDs,
timestamp cutoffs, and model provenance. It requires the LLM and LSTM
model identifiers to exist in the training partition for that ticker.

The result is marked `experimental_not_activated`. It returns a candidate
fused close alongside the LSTM close and provenance. It does not itself
publish a model pointer, alter active artifacts, or connect to the standard
forecast function.

==================== PAGE 067 OF 100 ====================
STAGED-TRIAL EVALUATION GATE

`assess_fusion_staged_trial` evaluates predefined checks: all configured
horizons are represented; validation does not regress versus LSTM on MAE
or directional accuracy; test macro MAE beats both LSTM and persistence;
and no test ticker/horizon regresses versus LSTM on MAE or direction.

A passing result means only that a staged shadow trial may be considered.
The gate is an initial diagnostic, not a significance test, production
approval, or a substitute for evaluating many untouched periods.

==================== PAGE 068 OF 100 ====================
OPT-IN SHADOW DISPLAY

The LSTM forecast page supports shadow display when
`EQUIMASTER_FUSION_SHADOW_VERSION` names an immutable local fusion artifact.
It requests archived sentiment and structured LLM synthesis at the
forecast origin, builds a provenance-bearing request, and displays the
candidate in a separate experimental panel.

The shadow result does not change the current LSTM metric, combined
signal, chart, or exported forecast CSV. A legacy LSTM, unavailable
archives, disabled provider, or incompatible artifact produces a separate
status/error message rather than a substituted LSTM result.

==================== PAGE 069 OF 100 ====================
SAFETY OF PRODUCTION FORECASTS

Normal forecasts remain LSTM-based. LLM configuration is disabled by
default for provider use, and fusion shadow is separately opt-in by an
explicit artifact version. There is no active fusion pointer, automatic
promotion, or silent fallback that labels a baseline result as fusion.

This separation reduces accidental behavior changes but does not make
shadow estimates reliable by itself. The user should regard the fusion
candidate as experimental and should not use it as a sole basis for
financial decisions.

FUNDAMENTALS OVERVIEW

The Fundamentals page combines public-source company snapshots, selected
peer comparisons, ratio assessments, and financial statements. The
documented interface supports 96 parameters across valuation,
profitability, growth, debt, liquidity, assets, liabilities, cash flow,
efficiency, per-share measures, and ownership.

Supported parameter slots do not imply every company publishes every
value. Missing or inappropriate fields are displayed as unavailable.
Details include source, period, basis, units, and explanatory context where
available.

==================== PAGE 071 OF 100 ====================
FUNDAMENTALS SOURCES

The documented public sources include Screener company pages and Yahoo
Finance data. Screener provides statements, ratios, and ownership sections;
Yahoo supplies additional profile and market fields. The project states
that it does not access private Screener sections.

External HTML layouts and APIs can change. The refresh path records warnings
and source status. A failed complete refresh is designed to preserve the
previous snapshot rather than replace it with an empty result.

==================== PAGE 072 OF 100 ====================
FINANCIAL STATEMENTS

The Fundamentals section includes quarterly results, annual profit and
loss, balance sheets, cash flows, historical ratios, and shareholding
history where source data is available. Individual statements can be
exported for closer inspection.

Fiscal period labels and currency matter when comparing records. A
retrieval date is not the same as the disclosure date. The user should read
each statement with its period, reporting basis, and source metadata rather
than assuming all values describe the same fiscal interval.

==================== PAGE 073 OF 100 ====================
RATIO CATALOG

The catalog covers market valuation, profitability, growth, leverage,
liquidity, asset quality, cash conversion, efficiency, dividends, per-share
items, and ownership. Headline cards include market capitalization, P/E,
P/B, dividend yield, ROE, published ROCE, debt/equity, and trailing EPS
when available.

Definitions and data availability differ by company and provider.
Financial-sector companies require distinct interpretations for leverage,
liquidity, margins, and capital adequacy. Ratios should be read with their
underlying statement dates and units.

==================== PAGE 074 OF 100 ====================
DERIVED FINANCIAL METRICS

The guide documents annual derivations using matching fiscal end dates.
Calculated ROCE uses EBIT divided by average annual capital employed,
where capital employed is assets less current liabilities. ROIC uses
effective-tax NOPAT divided by a stated debt-plus-equity-minus-cash
denominator.

CAGR excludes trailing-twelve-month values and requires positive endpoints
over an actual fiscal-year span. Derived values can differ from published
ratios because definitions, period timing, and accounting basis may differ.
The interface should identify calculated values and their formula.

==================== PAGE 075 OF 100 ====================
FINANCIAL ASSESSMENTS AND RANGES

The app displays explicit ranges for its ratio assessments, such as P/E,
P/B, ROE/ROCE, debt/equity, and current ratio. The Fundamentals guide
clearly labels these as application screening heuristics rather than
universal standards endorsed by external sources.

A low valuation multiple alone does not imply a high-quality company or a
buy signal. Negative earnings or book values are not labeled as cheap
multiples. The assessment is a way to contextualize a value, not a complete
investment thesis.

==================== PAGE 076 OF 100 ====================
PEER COMPARISON

Peer valuation bands are calculated only when enough selected peers share
industry, accounting basis, metric source, and reported-period label. The
documented range uses 80% to 120% of peer median when at least two eligible
peers are present. Stale snapshots are excluded.

Comparability checks reduce misleading cross-company comparisons, but
sector classifications and peer selection still matter. Banks, insurers,
and lenders are exempt from generic industrial leverage and liquidity
thresholds because their balance sheets use different economics.

==================== PAGE 077 OF 100 ====================
CURRENCY AND UNITS

Monetary totals from Screener are identified as INR crore, with per-share
values displayed in their appropriate units. The guide documents separate
currency metadata for companies whose quotation currency differs from
financial statement currency. Verified USD statements are not relabeled
as INR.

No unsupported foreign-exchange conversion is invented. Ambiguous
cross-currency valuation multiples are withheld. Provider-specific
representations, such as Yahoo debt/equity supplied as a percentage, are
normalized according to the documented implementation.

==================== PAGE 078 OF 100 ====================
MISSING VALUES AND SECTOR LIMITATIONS

Missing financial values remain unavailable. Bank-specific information
such as NPA, NIM, CASA, capital adequacy, or pledged shares is not
fabricated when current sources do not provide it. Generic industrial
thresholds are disabled where the financial-sector business model makes
them inappropriate.

This approach trades completeness for more honest reporting. Examiners
should assess whether the interface clearly distinguishes unavailable,
unsupported, and zero values, because these states have different
meanings.

Recommendation and risk components are connected to the dashboard, but

==================== PAGE 082 OF 100 ====================
SIGNAL FORMULA

The project documentation states that forecast/news display and evaluation
share the formula `0.6 * tanh(predicted_return * 10) + 0.4 * sentiment`.
The nonlinear return term bounds the price component before combining it
with a sentiment score.

This is a fixed heuristic, not a learned or empirically proven optimal
weighting. Its presence provides consistent implementation between
display and evaluation; it does not demonstrate that the signal predicts
future returns better than either input alone.

REPRODUCIBILITY

Reproducibility relies on matching processed-data snapshots, feature order,
scalers, model versions, hashes, training parameters, split boundaries,
and evaluation files. The fusion artifact similarly stores dataset and
estimator hashes and split provenance.

External source changes can prevent exact reconstruction if historical
snapshots were not archived. Reproducible code cannot recreate missing
point-in-time evidence after the fact. The quality of versioned metadata
and archives is therefore a central project dependency.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS: MODEL QUALITY

The documented LSTM results show mixed comparisons with persistence.
Directional accuracy declines at longer horizons in the reported means.
Test interval coverage is materially below the nominal 90% goal,

The learned fusion model has not been established as superior. A passing

generalization. Improvements must be measured on genuinely untouched
periods and reported with uncertainty and appropriate baselines.

CONCLUSION

Equimaster-Ai is a substantial integrated financial research application
with a useful dashboard, model operations, fundamentals, news evidence,
and explicit evaluation and provenance mechanisms. Its strongest
engineering qualities include modular research surfaces, immutable model
bundles, chronological evaluation, cutoff-aware evidence handling, and
clear separation of experimental fusion from production forecasts.

Its most important unresolved questions are predictive validity, interval
calibration, regional news quality, archive sufficiency, and operational
reliability over time. Those questions should remain visible in any
examiner presentation.
