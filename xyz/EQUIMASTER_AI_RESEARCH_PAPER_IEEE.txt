================================================================================
EQUIMASTER-AI: POINT-IN-TIME NEWS RETRIEVAL AND MULTI-HORIZON LSTM FORECASTING
FOR FINANCIAL RESEARCH
================================================================================

ABSTRACT

    This paper describes the current Equimaster-AI research platform, which
    combines per-ticker Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) price forecasting with a
    timestamp-aware financial-news retrieval and sentiment pipeline. The
    platform supports Indian equities and direct forecasts at Day (1 session),
    Week (5), Month (22), and Quarter (63) horizons. Quarter is approximately
    three calendar months of trading sessions. Historical
    market models use 60-session input
    windows, fit-only feature scaling, chronological calibration and test
    periods, and immutable versioned artifacts. News is retrieved from
    timestamped articles, scored by a keyword method by default, and optionally
    scored by FinBERT when explicitly enabled. An evidence-grounded language
    model synthesis is an optional capability; it is not presented as a
    validated price forecaster.

    The current saved evaluation contains 228 ticker-horizon results across
    57 active models; two of the 59 configured stock/index models have no
    bundle because the fresh-news training requirement was not met. Mean MAPE
    is 1.286%, 2.873%, 6.555%, and 10.054% at Day, Week, Month, and Quarter
    (1, 5, 22, and 63 sessions); persistence MAPE is 1.281%, 2.854%, 6.382%,
    and 9.385%. LSTM MAE is lower than persistence for 16/57, 20/57, 15/57,
    and 18/57 tickers at those horizons. Empirical interval coverage is
    76.40%, 76.05%, 69.03%, and 53.76% against a 90% nominal target. These
    observations do not establish a profitable trading strategy or show that news retrieval increases price
    accuracy. The historical price backtests use one fitted model per ticker
    and horizon with rolling observed input windows; they are not a repeated
    walk-forward retraining study. A separate expert-labeled financial
    sentence benchmark measures sentiment classification, not stock-return
    prediction. This paper reports the evidence available in the repository
    and distinguishes measured results from future research proposals.

    Index Terms: financial forecasting, LSTM, retrieval-augmented generation,
    RAG, financial sentiment, point-in-time data, time-series evaluation,
    Indian equities.

================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

    1.1 Background

    Financial prices are noisy, non-stationary, and affected by market-wide,
    sector-specific, company-specific, and event-driven information. A
    sequence model can learn temporal patterns in historical features, while
    news retrieval can expose company-relevant textual evidence to an analyst.
    These data sources have different roles: an LSTM produces a numerical
    forecast; retrieval locates supporting documents; a sentiment model
    estimates the polarity of text. Retrieval or fluent language generation
    does not by itself establish that a future price move is predictable.

    Equimaster-AI is a Streamlit-based research application for Indian listed
    equities. It joins market data, technical analysis, company fundamentals,
    news evidence, price forecasts, and model evaluation in one application.
    This paper focuses on the implemented LSTM and news/RAG components and
    their evaluation, rather than treating the application interface as
    evidence of predictive performance.

    1.2 Research questions

    This implementation and its currently available artifacts support the
    following questions:

    RQ1: What held-out price and directional metrics are measured for the
         current per-ticker LSTM models at four trading-session horizons?

    RQ2: How does the LSTM's price error compare with a no-change persistence
         price forecast?

    RQ3: What has been measured about the news sentiment component, and does
         the available evidence establish an improvement in stock-price
         forecasting from combining news with the LSTM?

    RQ4: Which evaluation and data-collection steps are needed before a
         stronger claim about directional accuracy or trading utility can be
         made?

    1.3 Contributions and scope

    This paper contributes:

    (1) An implementation-grounded account of the current forecast contract:
        prediction after the latest available close, with future-close targets
        Day (1), Week (5), Month (22), or Quarter (63) trading sessions ahead.
        Quarter is approximately three calendar months.

    (2) A description of per-ticker, per-horizon residual LSTMs, chronological
        fitting and testing, persisted backtest rows, and the persistence
        baseline.

    (3) A description of the timestamp-aware news retrieval and sentiment
        path, including its default keyword scorer, optional FinBERT scorer,
        and optional evidence-grounded language-model synthesis.

    (4) A report of the saved 57-ticker model results and the independently
        labeled sentence-level sentiment benchmark, with their limitations.

    (5) A research protocol for testing whether sentiment/RAG actually adds
        out-of-sample directional information.

    The work does not claim a production trading edge, calibrated confidence,
    statistically significant excess return, or a measured gain from RAG
    fusion. No such conclusion follows from the tests or metrics currently
    saved in the repository.

    1.4 Paper organization

    Section 2 summarizes relevant concepts. Section 3 describes data,
    forecasting, news retrieval, and fusion. Section 4 describes the software
    and reproducibility design. Section 5 reports available results. Section
    6 discusses interpretation and limitations. Section 7 proposes an
    accuracy-focused research plan. Section 8 concludes.

================================================================================
2. RELATED CONCEPTS
================================================================================

    2.1 Recurrent sequence models

    Long Short-Term Memory networks were introduced to address optimization
    difficulties associated with long dependencies in recurrent neural
    networks [1]. LSTMs are one possible model family for financial sequences;
    their use does not imply that prices contain stable, exploitable
    dependencies. Financial forecast evaluation therefore needs chronological
    holdouts and simple baselines in addition to model-fitting loss.

    2.2 Financial language and sentiment

    FinBERT adapts BERT-style language modeling to financial text [2].
    Sentiment classification and return forecasting are distinct tasks:
    correct identification of positive or negative language does not prove
    that the text predicts the sign or magnitude of a stock's future return.
    Class prevalence, event timing, market expectations, and the relationship
    between an article and a specific ticker all affect the usefulness of a
    sentiment feature.

    2.3 Retrieval-augmented systems

    Retrieval-augmented generation combines retrieved information with
    language-model generation [3]. For financial research, retrieval can
    provide traceable evidence and reduce reliance on unsupported recall.
    However, retrieval relevance, factual grounding, sentiment accuracy, and
    return-prediction value must each be evaluated separately. High retrieval
    relevance is not a forecast-accuracy metric.

    2.4 Time-aware evaluation

    Random train/test partitioning is generally unsuitable for forecasting
    when it allows future observations to influence earlier predictions.
    Chronological separation, purging overlapping multi-step targets, fitting
    preprocessing only on training rows, and point-in-time availability of
    news help reduce leakage. A fixed-model test and a repeated walk-forward
    simulation answer different questions; results from one should not be
    described as results from the other [4].

================================================================================
3. METHODS
================================================================================

    3.1 Forecast task and universe

    The canonical forecast task is an after-close prediction from the latest
    available close, with a future closing-price target at horizon h in
    {1, 5, 22, 63} trading sessions, displayed as Day, Week, Month, and
    Quarter. Quarter is approximately three calendar months. A ticker's latest close is the forecast
    origin. Calendar days, weekends, and market holidays are not counted as
    trading sessions.

    The current saved watchlist evaluation covers 57 ticker datasets, with
    one separately trained model for each available ticker-horizon pair.
    Therefore, the implementation is not one pooled LSTM trained jointly
    across all stocks. The reported evaluation has 228 ticker-horizon rows.
    Test-sample totals vary by horizon because the target horizon and available
    history affect the number of valid samples.

    3.2 Market data and feature preparation

    The application downloads and processes daily market histories using the
    repository's data pipeline. The canonical trainer loads each ticker's
    processed CSV and uses its available numeric columns as model features.
    The exact feature list is recorded with each immutable model bundle;
    consequently, this paper does not assume that every ticker has an
    identical fixed-width set of 14 features.

    Model inputs are sequences of 60 consecutive rows. The forecast target is
    the processed Close column. Duplicate dates and non-finite feature values
    are rejected by the trainer. A Min-Max scaler is fit on the fitting
    portion only and then applied to the later calibration and test rows.
    This prevents the scaler from learning test-period extrema.

    3.3 Chronological training and target separation

    The trainer chooses a chronological test boundary using the available
    history and a default 0.8 training fraction after the initial lookback.
    It creates an earlier calibration boundary within the pre-test history.
    The fitting, calibration, and test data are not randomly shuffled.
    Multi-session labels crossing split boundaries are excluded by the
    sequence-splitting routine, so training target dates do not overlap the
    held-out test target period.

    The calibration segment is used for validation loss and early stopping.
    The test segment is used for the reported final test metrics. Because
    calibration also selects early-stopping weights, the residual-based
    forecast intervals are empirical validation intervals and are not
    claimed to have guaranteed conformal coverage.

    Each training run records data and model hashes, feature order, dates,
    training parameters, dependency versions, metrics, and backtest rows in
    an immutable versioned bundle. An atomic active-model pointer selects a
    complete model/scaler/snapshot bundle.

    3.4 LSTM architecture and objective

    The current canonical model uses a 60-step input sequence and two
    recurrent layers of 50 LSTM units each. The first LSTM returns its full
    sequence; the second returns its final representation. Dropout of 0.2 is
    used after each LSTM layer, followed by a 25-unit dense layer and an
    output layer with h values for a direct horizon-h forecast.

    For the canonical close-forecast model, the output is a residual
    correction added to the last observed feature row's close value. The
    model is compiled with the Adam optimizer and mean-squared error loss.
    Training uses batch size 32, a fixed random seed, and early stopping on
    calibration loss with patience 3 and restoration of the best weights.
    Epoch count is configurable; these details are recorded in model
    metadata. The current objective is a price-regression objective, not a
    classification loss optimized directly for up/down accuracy.

    For a horizon h, the network produces the next h closing values and the
    saved point forecast and primary evaluation use the final value at that
    horizon. Intermediate predicted values are not treated as independent
    model forecasts at every requested horizon.

    3.5 Price and direction metrics

    Given actual target price y_i and predicted price yhat_i:

        MAE  = mean_i |yhat_i - y_i|
        RMSE = sqrt(mean_i (yhat_i - y_i)^2)
        MAPE = 100 * mean_i (|yhat_i - y_i| / |y_i|)

    MAPE is a percentage price error; it is not a percentage of correct
    predictions and must not be converted to "accuracy" by subtracting it
    from 100%.

    Given origin close p_i, actual target close y_i, and predicted target
    close yhat_i, the reported directional hit rate compares the signs of:

        actual_return_i    = (y_i - p_i) / p_i
        predicted_return_i = (yhat_i - p_i) / p_i

    The reported directional accuracy is the mean of matching direction
    indicators. The existing scoring helper treats an exactly zero predicted
    or actual return specially: a zero prediction is counted correct only
    when the actual return is also exactly zero. As a result, the
    no-change-price forecast is a meaningful MAE/RMSE/MAPE baseline, but its
    stored directional score is not a useful majority-direction classifier
    baseline. Future direction studies should report a separately defined
    up/down classifier baseline and specify how zero-return and neutral cases
    are handled.

    3.6 Persistence price baseline

    The persistence forecast sets the future price equal to the current close:

        yhat_persistence_i = p_i

    LSTM and persistence regression errors are compared on the same held-out
    targets. The "models beating persistence MAE" count in Section 5 counts
    ticker-level comparisons where the LSTM MAE is strictly lower than that
    ticker's persistence MAE. It is not a count of profitable models and
    does not adjust for transaction costs, risk, or statistical uncertainty.

    3.7 News collection, retrieval, and scoring

    News sources are selected and validated by the news-fetching pipeline.
    Article records can include title, summary, content, source, URL,
    publication time, observation time, and ticker metadata. Runtime news is
    cached. The configured recent-news window is two days and the maximum
    article count is eight by default.

    The NewsRetriever chunks articles and ranks chunks for a query. It uses
    an embedding-backed retrieval path when the embedding model/index is
    available; otherwise it uses TF-IDF with unigrams and bigrams and cosine
    similarity. It returns up to five chunks by default, subject to the
    requested article limit. The code supports named queries such as recent
    sentiment, negative catalysts, bullish momentum, and risk events.

    For point-in-time queries, articles must have a parseable publication time
    no later than the forecast origin. The retriever also requires observation
    time when an as-of cutoff is provided and rejects articles observed after
    that cutoff. The fusion-dataset contract independently validates that
    both publication and observation timestamps are no later than the
    forecast origin. These controls are necessary to avoid using information
    that was not available when the prediction would have been made.

    The default sentiment method is a keyword scorer. It counts configured
    positive and negative terms and scales the difference by text length.
    FinBERT scoring is optional and requires explicit environment
    configuration; it is not the default scorer. Retrieved-text scores are
    adjusted by relevance, aggregated with source/relevance weights, and
    exposed with article evidence. Confidence, impact, and source weights
    are heuristic quantities, not calibrated probabilities.

    3.8 LLM synthesis and forecast/news fusion

    An optional language-model provider can synthesize retrieved evidence.
    Fusion records identify the LLM and LSTM versions and link generated
    claims to evidence identifiers. The validation contract rejects missing
    or post-origin evidence and invalid citations. These are data-contract
    safeguards; they do not demonstrate predictive value.

    The shared forecast/news signal formula is:

        forecast_signal(r) = clip(tanh(10 r), -1, 1)
        combined_signal    = 0.6 forecast_signal(r) + 0.4 sentiment

    where r is the LSTM predicted return from the origin close and sentiment
    lies in [-1, 1]. This is a fixed rule, not a learned fusion model. The
    formula is used for displayed forecast/news fusion and evaluation code.
    The current saved per-ticker LSTM backtests evaluate price forecasts
    without mixing in today's news. Measuring historical news fusion requires
    timestamped articles archived as they were available at each historical
    forecast origin. The repository contains point-in-time fusion schemas
    and fixed-rule baseline evaluation code, but the saved watchlist price
    evaluation is not an LSTM-plus-RAG return-accuracy experiment.

    3.9 News-sentiment benchmark

    The repository includes an evaluation on 2,264 expert-labeled English
    financial sentences from the FinancialPhraseBank all-agree subset. This
    evaluates sentence-level positive/neutral/negative classification. It
    does not evaluate article retrieval quality on real-time Indian-market
    data, next-session stock direction, or portfolio returns. Benchmark
    provenance, source revision, license, and archive checksum are recorded
    in the repository.

4. IMPLEMENTATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY
================================================================================

    4.1 Components

    The relevant implementation surfaces are:

    • src/training.py: canonical per-ticker chronological model trainer,
      model evaluation, versioned bundles, and backtest exports.
    • src/train_model.py: LSTM model architecture and objective.
    • src/evaluate_predictions.py: regression and directional metrics.
    • src/prediction_contract.py: signal and fusion contract.
    • src/news/fetcher.py: article collection, timestamp normalization, and
      archived article loading.
    • src/news/retriever.py: timestamp-aware chunk retrieval with embedding
      and TF-IDF paths.
    • src/news/sentiment.py: default keyword scoring and optional FinBERT.
    • src/fusion_dataset.py and src/fusion_evaluation.py: point-in-time
      fusion data validation and fixed-rule baseline evaluation.
    • scripts/evaluate_lstm.py and scripts/evaluate_watchlist.py: held-out
      model evaluation entrypoints.

    4.2 Commands to reproduce evaluations

    Evaluate one saved model and its held-out backtest:

        python scripts/evaluate_lstm.py --ticker TCS.NS --horizon 1

    Evaluate all available tickers and four horizons, writing results to a
    separate file so the current summary is preserved:

        python scripts/evaluate_watchlist.py --horizons 1,5,22,63 --output data/evaluation/quarter63_research_run.json

    Evaluate the configured labeled-news fixture:

        python scripts/evaluate_news.py

    Run the offline test suite:

        python -m pytest tests -q

    Reproduction requires the matching processed data and versioned model
    bundles. Model bundles record the fitted data snapshot and hashes.
    The evaluation command should fail when a requested verified artifact is
    unavailable rather than treating a legacy model as if it had a verified
    chronological test contract.

    4.3 Scope of software tests

    Software tests verify contracts, parsing, timestamp cutoffs, model
    artifacts, dashboard behavior, and metric computation. Passing software
    tests is evidence of implementation behavior; it is not evidence that a
    forecast is accurate or profitable.

    5.2 Interpretation of price and directional metrics

    The Day mean MAPE of 1.286% describes average absolute percentage
    price error under the stated test protocol. It does not mean that 98.74%
    of predictions are "accurate." The corresponding mean directional
    accuracy is 30.13%. Directional accuracy is below 50% at every horizon
    under the saved metric definition.

    The persistence comparison reinforces that the LSTM does not uniformly
    improve price error. The LSTM has lower MAE for fewer than half of the
    evaluated tickers at every horizon. These results justify per-ticker and per-horizon model
    selection, but that selection itself must be based on validation data,
    not repeated reuse of the final test period.

    The reported 90% nominal interval target is not reached in the saved
    average test coverage at any listed horizon. Coverage is particularly low
    at the Quarter (63 sessions, approximately three months). These intervals are based on validation residual
    quantiles, and calibration-period early stopping means they should be
    described as empirical intervals, not guaranteed coverage.

    5.3 Frozen-holdout cost sensitivity

    A fixed long-only rule was applied to non-overlapping holdout forecasts:
    enter when predicted return exceeds an illustrative round-trip cost, and
    otherwise remain in cash. At 10 basis points, mean realized net return
    per trade was -0.142% for Day (647 trades), +0.034% for Week (624),
    -0.594% for Month (260), and -1.794% for Quarter (111). Trades are
    equally weighted; cash return is zero. The small positive Week mean has
    no uncertainty estimate and does not establish an economic edge. The
    scenarios omit broker-specific taxes, slippage, liquidity, financing,
    portfolio sizing, and concurrent capital allocation. Models were frozen
    within their test periods; this is not a refitted walk-forward study.
    The complete 0/10/25/50 basis-point report is saved in
    data/evaluation/cost-sensitivity-20261026.json.

    5.4 News classification benchmark

    On 2,264 expert-labeled FinancialPhraseBank sentences, the current
    keyword-based evaluation reports:

    Metric                                  Result
    ------------------------------------------------
    Accuracy                                76.19%
    Majority-class accuracy baseline       61.44%
    Balanced accuracy                       60.16%
    Macro F1                                0.626
    Negative-class recall                   27.06%
    Heuristic-confidence ECE                0.240

    Class counts are 1,391 neutral, 570 positive, and 303 negative. The
    accuracy exceeds the majority-class baseline, but balanced accuracy and
    negative recall reveal an important weakness on minority classes. The
    confidence estimate is heuristic and is not well calibrated by this
    measurement. These are text-label results; they must not be presented as
    price-direction accuracy.

    Retrieval Recall@1 of 1.0 was observed only on two explicitly synthetic
    smoke queries. This is a functionality check, not a representative
    real-world retrieval benchmark. No retrieved-news experiment in the
    saved model summary establishes an increase in stock forecast accuracy.

    5.4 Fusion evidence

    The repository implements a point-in-time fusion record contract and
    fixed-rule evaluation functions for LSTM, news sentiment, and optional
    LLM stance comparisons. A valid fusion dataset requires timestamped
    evidence available by each forecast origin. The current saved watchlist
    results do not include that historical evidence aligned to every market
    test row. Accordingly, this paper reports no measured LSTM-plus-RAG
    directional-accuracy gain.

================================================================================
6. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

    6.1 What the results support

    The evidence supports the following limited conclusions:

    (a) The application can train and evaluate chronological, per-ticker
        multi-horizon LSTM artifacts and persist reproducible evaluation
        records.

    (b) The tested models have 30.13% and 34.12% mean directional accuracy
        at one and five sessions under the saved metric; longer-horizon
        averages are 36.30% and 41.21%.

    (c) The LSTM's price-error performance is mixed against a no-change
        persistence baseline and differs by ticker and horizon.

    (d) The keyword news scorer achieves 76.19% accuracy on one
        sentence-level benchmark, but negative-class recall remains 27.06%
        and confidence calibration remains weak.

    (e) The software supports evidence retrieval, timestamp checks, and
        optional language-model synthesis, but these system capabilities
        alone do not show that retrieved news improves future-price forecasts.

    6.2 Important limitations

    (a) Fixed fitted models: Historical backtests roll observed prior input
        windows through a fixed fitted model. They do not repeatedly refit
        during the test period. Performance under recurring retraining and
        realistic model deployment may differ.

    (b) Limited market history and regime dependence: Each ticker has a
        finite historical sample. Market regimes, corporate actions,
        volatility, liquidity, and changes in data quality can alter
        performance. Aggregate means can hide ticker-level variation.

    (c) Objective mismatch: The LSTM minimizes normalized-price MSE, whereas
        directional accuracy measures sign agreement. MSE improvements need
        not increase up/down hit rate.

    (d) Direction baseline definition: The saved no-change price forecast is
        suitable for regression-error comparison. Its zero predicted return
        is not a useful directional classifier under the current sign-matching
        metric. A separately specified majority-class or momentum classifier
        should be used in direction studies.

    (e) News label mismatch: Sentence sentiment labels do not encode the
        direction or magnitude of future returns. The FinancialPhraseBank
        corpus is English financial text and is not an Indian-equity event
        return dataset.

    (f) Historical news coverage: A reliable fusion backtest requires news
        publication and observation timestamps, point-in-time article
        snapshots, ticker/entity relevance, and labels aligned to the market
        forecast origin. Missing historical articles cannot be replaced with
        present-day news without look-ahead bias.

    (g) Heuristic fusion: The 0.6/0.4 signal formula is fixed rather than
        fitted and validated as a directional classifier. Sentiment,
        relevance, impact, and confidence are not necessarily calibrated.

    (h) Trading costs and execution: Forecast metrics do not account for
        transaction costs, taxes, bid-ask spreads, slippage, position sizing,
        market impact, short-sale constraints, or risk-adjusted returns.
        Directional accuracy is not equivalent to investment performance.

    (i) Statistical uncertainty: The reported table contains descriptive
        means and win counts. It does not include uncertainty intervals,
        paired significance tests, or corrections for testing many tickers,
        horizons, and configurations.

    (j) Interval coverage: Current residual intervals under-cover their
        nominal 90% target on the saved test data, especially at longer
        horizons. They should not be interpreted as calibrated probabilities.

    6.3 Threats to validity

    Model comparisons can be biased by repeatedly choosing model settings
    after observing test results. Repeated overlapping forecast origins
    induce dependence between samples, and taking a macro-average across
    tickers weights a small-history ticker equally with a long-history
    ticker. The exact date range and result depend on the saved processed
    data and model version. Reproduction should retain the immutable bundle,
    source snapshot, and evaluation output together.

================================================================================
7. RESEARCH PLAN FOR IMPROVING DIRECTIONAL ACCURACY
================================================================================

    The current results do not justify claiming a higher-accuracy model.
    Improvements should be earned through controlled experiments with a
    locked final test period.

    7.1 First, establish comparable direction baselines

    Before changing architecture, specify the classification target and
    compare the model with:

    (1) Always-up / majority-class prediction based on the training period.
    (2) A simple recent-return momentum rule.
    (3) A regularized logistic classifier on lagged returns and technical
        features.
    (4) The current LSTM translated to up/down probabilities.

    Report balanced accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC where appropriate,
    and a confusion matrix. Define a neutral band for economically negligible
    moves before examining test results. Report both coverage (fraction of
    dates on which the system issues a non-neutral prediction) and hit rate
    conditional on making a prediction; abstaining can raise the latter while
    reducing prediction coverage.

    7.2 Align the training objective with the desired metric

    If the product goal is up/down direction, experiment with a return-sign
    classification target or a joint objective combining return regression
    and direction classification. Select thresholds and class weights on
    training/validation data only. Compare against the regression model on
    identical forecast origins. Do not infer improvement from lower training
    loss; report held-out directional metrics.

    7.3 Test LSTM and RAG as an ablation

    Build a timestamped dataset with one row per ticker, forecast origin, and
    horizon. Preserve the exact LSTM forecast available at that origin and
    only news articles both published and observed by that time. Then compare:

    A. Persistence and simple market baselines.
    B. LSTM-only.
    C. News sentiment-only.
    D. LSTM plus keyword/news features.
    E. LSTM plus FinBERT features, if enabled and benchmarked.
    F. LSTM plus cited LLM stance/evidence features, if available.

    All models must use the same rows, horizons, and chronological splits.
    Train any fusion weights on earlier dates and freeze them before the
    final test. Report per-horizon and per-ticker results, paired differences,
    uncertainty estimates that account for temporal dependence, and sample
    counts. If historical news is missing, report the reduced sample and
    compare all ablation arms on that same subset.

    7.4 Use walk-forward evaluation

    After a preliminary fixed test, simulate deployment by repeatedly:
    fitting on past data, selecting settings on a later validation segment,
    forecasting the next block, then advancing the origin. Use purged
    boundaries for overlapping multi-session targets. Keep a final untouched
    period for a single confirmatory evaluation. Record every experiment to
    avoid selecting a result from many untracked trials.

    7.5 Improve data before increasing model complexity

    Audit adjusted prices, corporate actions, missing rows, volume anomalies,
    ticker mappings, and feature availability dates. Add market and sector
    context only when historical values are aligned to each forecast origin.
    Normalize return-based features using training data only. News features
    should include freshness, article count, source, entity relevance, and
    disagreement, but should not leak future revisions or labels.

    7.6 Model selection, uncertainty, and economic evaluation

    Use validation-only model selection; avoid selecting a separate winner
    for every ticker based on final test performance. Evaluate uncertainty
    coverage on untouched dates and recalibrate intervals on a disjoint
    calibration period. If the intended use is trading, test an explicit
    decision rule with realistic costs, turnover, exposure, drawdown, and
    risk-adjusted metrics in addition to forecast accuracy. A statistically
    better direction score may still be economically unusable.

================================================================================
8. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

    The current Equimaster-AI implementation is a financial research platform
    containing per-ticker LSTM close forecasts and a timestamp-aware
    news-retrieval/sentiment path. Its saved 57-model evaluation reports
    mean directional accuracy of 30.13% at one session, 34.12% at five,
    36.30% at 22, and 41.21% at 63 sessions. Its LSTM beats persistence MAE
    for 16, 20, 15, and 18 tickers at those respective horizons. Two of the
    59 configured models remain unavailable because the fresh-news training
    requirement is unmet. The sentence-level news benchmark reports 76.19%
    accuracy, but that benchmark
    does not measure stock-price direction and also reveals weak negative
    recall.

    These findings do not demonstrate substantially higher directional
    accuracy, a trading edge, or incremental value from RAG. The next
    scientifically defensible step is not to claim an improvement but to
    collect point-in-time historical news aligned to forecast origins and
    run a controlled LSTM-only versus LSTM-plus-news walk-forward ablation,
    with clear direction baselines and a locked test period. Any model change
    should be retained only if the improvement repeats on unseen periods and
    remains meaningful after uncertainty and trading costs are considered.
